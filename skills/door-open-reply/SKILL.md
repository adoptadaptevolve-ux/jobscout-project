# Door-Open Reply

Turn a "no", "not yet", or "maybe something else" into a reply that leaves the
sender glad they wrote, and makes the next step feel already agreed.

## What you need

1. **The email they received.** If it isn't pasted, ask for it.
2. **What they want to say back.** They may give rough notes ("thank her, say
   I'd consider the other role, ask for the description"). Use those as the
   spine. If they give nothing, infer the most useful goal from the email and
   say what you assumed.

Don't ask more questions than that. Fill small gaps from context and note any
assumption in one line after the draft.

## Read the email for openings

Before writing, find:

- **Who sent it, and their name and title.** Use their first name if they
  signed with it.
- **The door they left open.** Look for "you may be a good fit for...", "keep in
  touch", "future openings", "not right now", "reach out again", or a suggested
  role, team, or timeline. This is the hook the reply builds on.
- **Anything specific worth thanking.** Their time, an interview, feedback,
  what you learned about the company, their transparency or speed.
- **Their tone.** Match it: formal stays formal, and casual can be a bit warmer.

## How to build the reply

Write in this order. Each part is usually one or two sentences.

1. **Thanks, made specific.** Name what you're grateful for, such as their
   time, the conversations, or the transparency. Never say just "thanks for
   the update."
2. **Positive takeaway.** One line on what the user valued or learned about the
   company or team. This shows genuine interest without sounding hurt.
3. **Gentle leverage (optional).** If the user says it's true, mention other
   conversations or momentum ("I have a few conversations underway over the
   coming weeks"). This signals they are in demand. Never invent offers,
   interviews, or deadlines. Only include this if the user said it.
4. **The pivot.** Pick up the door they left open and state real interest:
   "The [role] you mentioned sounds like a strong fit, and I'd absolutely
   consider it."
5. **Assumptive next step.** Ask for the concrete thing and state what the
   user will do with it, as if it's already happening:
   - Good: "Please send the description my way, and I'll get my application
     in once it's complete."
   - Avoid: "If you think it might make sense, maybe you could possibly send..."
   The ask is clear and confident, and the user's follow-through is promised.
6. **Warm close.** One short line, then the sign-off.

## Style rules

- **Keep it short.** Under 150 words for the body, because a recruiter reads it
  in 20 seconds.
- **Use confident, optimistic, plain language.** Use contractions. No
  groveling ("I completely understand if...", "sorry to bother"), no
  disappointment, and no arguing with the decision.
- **Cut filler openers** like "I hope this email finds you well" and "Just
  wanted to reach out."
- **Use active verbs.** Prefer "I'd welcome", "I'll", and "please send" over
  hedges like "I was wondering if perhaps."
- **Write in the user's voice.** If a personal writing-style skill or voice
  profile is available, apply it.
- **Stay honest.** Persuasion comes from framing and confidence, never from
  false claims.

## Output

1. **Subject line**, only if the reply starts a new thread. Otherwise write
   "(reply in thread)".
2. **The draft**, ready to paste.
3. **One alternative**, either shorter or warmer, labeled.
4. **One line** listing any assumption you made, such as a name or the role
   title.

## Example

**Received:** "Thanks for interviewing with us. We've decided to move forward
with other candidates for the Operations Manager role. That said, your
background may be a great fit for our Program Coordinator position. You're
always welcome to apply for future roles. - Dana"

**User's notes:** thank her, appreciated learning about the company, have other
conversations in the coming weeks, but would consider the suggested role, ask
her to send the description and say I'll let her know once I've applied.

**Draft:**

> Hi Dana,
>
> Thank you for your time throughout the process. I really enjoyed learning
> more about the company, and I appreciate the transparency you and the team
> have shown at every step.
>
> I have a few other conversations underway over the coming weeks. Even so,
> I'd absolutely consider the Program Coordinator role you mentioned, because
> it sounds like a strong fit. Please send the description my way, and I'll
> let you know as soon as my application is complete.
>
> Thanks again, and I look forward to staying in touch.
>
> Best,
> [Name]

## Works beyond recruiting

Use the same structure for:

- A prospect's "not right now"
- A client declining a proposal
- A networking contact who redirects you
- A vendor or partner pause

Swap "role" for the product, proposal, or project the sender pointed toward.
